Hasebul Hassan Chowdhury

Software engineer · problem solver · mentor

I am a software engineer in Dhaka. Most days I keep the websites of a Norwegian news group fast, secure and online; in between, I build AI agents that take on parts of that work, and I study how far such agents can be trusted to run real systems.

For more than four years at Cefalo, a Norwegian software company with over 200 people, I have worked inside the product teams of its clients: DN Media Group, the Oslo media group that has published business journalism since 1889; Stiftelsen Asta, the foundation set up by Norway’s archives association and the National Archivist, which describes itself as Norway’s leading archive service provider; Zaui, a hotel-software company with teams in Canada, the US and Norway; and, for a short period, Kongsberg, the international Norwegian technology group serving the maritime, energy, defence and aerospace industries. I also worked on Cefalo ATS, the internal applicant tracking system Cefalo Bangladesh uses to manage job applications, candidate evaluation and recruitment. Working inside a client’s own team means sharing its goals, its deadlines and its users, not just handing over code.

I am one of Cefalo’s first 15 Claude Certified Architects, and I helped colleagues prepare for the exam by mentoring them and putting together a study curriculum and practice tests. I have also co-authored three applied machine learning research papers.

Why software engineering

Competitive programming pulled me in. At AIUB I solved contest problems, more than 2,100 of them over the years, and competed with my team in the Asia Dhaka Regional preliminary contests, which made me love problem solving. During my student years I also built software for fellow students, and I was always curious about games and computer hardware: how a machine actually runs the code we write. That curiosity reached the AIUB Computer Club too, which recognised my contribution to its Cyber Gaming Fest in 2022.

Later, the rise of AI made the field even more interesting. Today I build AI-assisted tools into my own engineering work, and I want to understand how AI agents can help run real systems safely.

What I care about

  • System design: shaping software so it stays simple to change as it grows.
  • Distributed systems: services that keep working together across networks, failures and load.
  • Cloud: building and running software on cloud platforms in a way that stays simple, secure and affordable.
  • Site reliability engineering: keeping systems fast and available, and learning from every incident so it does not happen twice.
  • Cybersecurity: building software that is secure by default, from code to infrastructure.
  • Data privacy: handling people’s data carefully, collecting only what is needed and keeping it protected.
  • Agentic AI: AI agents that plan and act on real tasks, not just answer questions.
  • Cloud agents: AI agents that help people run cloud and software systems safely, with a person in control of risky steps.
  • LLMs: how large language models work, and how to use them reliably in real products.
  • Harness engineering: the tools, guardrails and context around an AI agent that decide how well and how safely it works.
